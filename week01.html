Week 01

The Beginning of the Journey - Principles and Practices, Project Management

Introduction

This assignment introduces the foundation of Fab Academy. During this first week, we began to understand how the program works, the real importance of documenting every week, and the continuous record of our learning process. This project focuses on creating and finding new ways to innovate and build real solutions to the problems we identified throughout this process.

1. The Beginning

Think to Create

Every solution starts with a problem worth solving. Water is one of the most valuable resources in agriculture, yet a large part of it is wasted through inefficient irrigation, evaporation, and watering without real knowledge of what the plants actually need.

At the same time, growing our own food is becoming more important than ever. Self-sufficiency in agriculture means producing more with less: less water, less space, and less dependence on outside supplies. This is the need that guides our first ideas, and the starting point for everything we will design and build in the coming weeks.

8. Process for Obtaining Ideas

Comparing Alternatives

Our team aims to research, design, and evaluate different alternatives of products that can be manufactured with 3D printing technologies, in order to efficiently solve a technical and functional problem found in everyday life.

Our main motivation is to take advantage of the flexibility, precision, and speed of additive manufacturing and parametric design. Through this ideation process, we look for real user needs, iterate solutions through rapid prototyping, and develop a product that is viable and scalable from an engineering point of view, adds significant value, optimizes resources, and improves people's quality of life.

Figure 7. The three ideas considered by the team.

Option A: Smart Temperature-Regulating Cup

A cup or thermos with a 3D-printed shell that integrates temperature sensors and thermoelectric components (such as Peltier cells) to heat or cool the drink to the exact temperature the user chooses.

Pros: It solves a very common everyday annoyance (drinks losing their ideal temperature) and is an excellent project to apply thermodynamics, thermal insulation, and power electronics in a small space.

Cons: Combining liquids, electricity, and heat demands perfect waterproofing. Common 3D-printing plastics such as PLA or PETG can deform or melt at high temperatures, so more expensive technical materials would be required.

Option B: Anti-Stress Music Therapy Device

An interactive desktop device designed for university life. It combines touch buttons, LED light patterns, and white noise, binaural frequencies, or relaxing music to help reduce anxiety and improve concentration during exam periods.

Pros: It has a strong focus on mental health and student well-being. The electronics are low-voltage and very safe to implement, and the 3D-printed shell allows organic, compact, and creative designs that use little filament.

Cons: It is hard to objectively measure how effective the product is on the user's stress level. The hardware also competes directly with free mobile apps (such as Spotify or Calm), so the physical design must justify its use.

Option C: Smart Hanging Planter (IoT)

A system of hanging planters with hidden 3D-printed compartments, equipped with microcontrollers and capacitive sensors. They monitor soil moisture and send the data to a web dashboard to prevent over- or under-watering.

Pros: It addresses the real, well-documented problem of houseplant mortality caused by lack of information. It is the most complete project, combining CAD design, additive manufacturing, IoT electronics, cloud databases, and web development.

Cons: The CAD design must be extremely precise to isolate water and wet soil from the electronics. Fitting the cables, battery, and board in a limited hanging volume requires several iterations and print tests.

Chosen Idea: Smart Vertical Farm

After comparing the alternatives and connecting them with the need we identified at the beginning (saving water and growing our own food), our final chosen idea is a smart vertical garden. It will use sensors to monitor soil humidity, temperature, and water level, so that each plant receives only what it really needs while using less space and less water.
